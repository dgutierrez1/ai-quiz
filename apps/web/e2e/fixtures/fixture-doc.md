# A Field Guide to Sourdough Bread

This guide covers the essentials of baking a naturally leavened sourdough
loaf, from selecting ingredients through the final bake. It is used **only**

## Ingredients

Sourdough bread needs only four ingredients: flour, water, salt, and a live
sourdough starter. Bread flour with a high protein content produces the
strongest gluten network. Filtered water avoids chlorine, which can slow
fermentation. Fine sea salt dissolves evenly through the dough.

## Fermentation

Fermentation is driven by wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria living in the
starter. Bulk fermentation typically runs four to six hours at room
temperature, during which the dough should roughly double in volume. A
warmer kitchen speeds fermentation; a cooler one slows it down, which is
why many bakers retard the dough in the refrigerator overnight for a more
complex flavor.

## Shaping

Shaping builds surface tension so the loaf holds its structure during the
final rise. A bench scraper helps drag the dough across an unfloured
section of the counter, tightening the outer skin without tearing it.

## Baking

Baking a sourdough loaf in a preheated Dutch oven traps steam, which keeps
the crust soft long enough for the loaf to fully expand before it sets.
The lid typically comes off partway through baking so the crust can brown
and turn crisp.
